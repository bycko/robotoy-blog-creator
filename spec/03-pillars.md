# 03 — Pillars

Every plan row and every article belongs to exactly one pillar. The pillar decides what the article is for, what the reader leaves with, and what makes it fail. Classify by that outcome, not by which words appear in the topic. How any article reads, in every pillar, is in [`09-editorial-guidelines.md`](09-editorial-guidelines.md). How Planner ranks and places rows is in [`13-planner.md`](13-planner.md).

Use the identifiers (`GUIDE`, `INSPIRATION`, `GIFT`, `COMMUNITY`) in the plan and in run files. The pillar is not shown on the page and is never written into the article text.

## Overview

| ID | Reader need | What belongs here | Who creates the row | Placement limits |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `GUIDE` | „Čo mám urobiť?“ | building, tools, glue, finishing, lighting, repairs, troubleshooting, choosing a first model | Planner or Editor | none |
| `INSPIRATION` | „Čo je na tom zaujímavé a čo si môžem všimnúť?“ | stories and history behind a model's real subject, how a mechanism works, a technique, the hobby as a way of spending time, displaying finished builds | Planner or Editor | none |
| `GIFT` | „Ako vyberiem niečo vhodné pre tohto človeka?“ | a gift guide tied to one international or commemorative day in the [holiday calendar](../calendar/README.md) | Planner or Editor | only inside that day's holiday window; **never in a week that holds another holiday row** |
| `COMMUNITY` | „Čo urobili iní a čo si z toho môžem vziať?“ | challenge results, build of the month, a builder's technique, what builders say | **Editor only**, with material in [`community/`](../community/README.md) | Creator skips the row when its material is missing |

**Planner never writes a `COMMUNITY` row.** It has no material and must not invent builders, photos, quotes, numbers, or results.

## The decision rule

The same subject can belong to different pillars. A music box:

- „Ako opraviť hraciu skrinku, ktorá sa zasekáva“ → `GUIDE`
- „Ako funguje mechanická hracia skrinka“ → `INSPIRATION`
- „Ako vybrať hraciu skrinku ako darček pre mamu“ → `GIFT`
- „Ako si čitateľ prerobil osvetlenie svojej hracej skrinky“ → `COMMUNITY`, and only with approved material

When unsure, finish this sentence: after reading, the reader can do, decide, understand, or notice **this**. That answer is the pillar. It is also what `must_answer` has to name ([`13-planner.md`](13-planner.md)).

## `GUIDE`

**Purpose.** Help a builder finish a build, solve a problem, avoid a mistake, choose, or finish better. The reader leaves able to do something they could not reliably do before. A guide does not only describe the correct result. It gives enough to reach it.

**Reader.** Someone holding a kit, or about to buy their first one: a beginner stuck at a step, an adult choosing a first model, a builder who wants a cleaner finish.

**Example topics:**

- Ako vybrať lepidlo na miniatúrny domček a koľko ho naozaj treba
- Zlomil sa ti drevený diel: ako ho opraviť a pokračovať v stavbe
- Mechanizmus dreveného modelu sa zasekáva: ako nájsť a odstrániť trenie

**A good article answers**, in this order where the problem needs it: what to do, what to inspect or compare, what the observation means, and what to try next. It also answers:

- what they need, including things most households already have;
- how long it takes and where beginners usually go wrong;
- how to tell that it worked, and what to try when it did not.

Not every step needs every part. A step the reader must act on still names the action, the sign to watch, and what that sign means. „Skontroluj posuvné časti“ fails. „Sleduj celú dráhu. Ak sa zadrhne vždy na tom istom mieste, pozri, či hrana nedrie o vedenie“ passes.

Where kits differ, give the principle, say what to look for, and send the reader to that kit's manual for the part the manuals do not share. A Robotoys guide never overrides the manufacturer's instructions. An irreversible step (glue, force, heat, removing material, changing wiring) comes after a reversible check when one exists.

**It fails when:**

- a step works only with one specific model ("vezmi diel B12 zo štvrtej dosky");
- the answer is "kúp si iný model" or "kúp si náradie" with no way forward using what the reader has;
- it restates the instruction booklet without adding why or what to watch for;
- it says „skontroluj“ without saying what to inspect, or names a cause with no way to recognise it;
- it gives an intervention before the problem is identified, or one universal fix where the manuals differ;
- it recommends an irreversible action before a simpler check;
- time, ease, or success is promised without a condition ("za jeden večer to zvládne každý");
- it teaches nothing a builder could not guess from the title.

## `INSPIRATION`

**Purpose.** Give a builder a reason to start, continue, or look at their build differently. The reader leaves knowing a story, an idea, or a way to enjoy the hobby.

**Reader.** Someone curious about the hobby, or a builder between projects: a reader who likes books, trains, history, music, or quiet evenings, and has not yet connected that interest with building.

**Example topics:**

- Book nook: ako sa kútik medzi knihami stal obľúbeným projektom čitateľov
- Parné lokomotívy: skutočný príbeh za drevenými modelmi vlakov
- Hotové modely na poličke: ako ich vystaviť, aby nezapadli prachom

**A good article answers** by connecting a fact or an observation to why it matters and to what the reader can notice on a model, a build, or a display. It also answers:

- what the real-world subject is, with facts that are true;
- one concrete thing the reader can notice or try.

„Parná lokomotíva prenáša pohyb piesta na kolesá cez ojnice. Keď sa na modeli hýbu s kolesami, nie sú len ozdoba“ passes. „Parné lokomotívy fascinujú ľudí už generácie“ fails.

**It fails when:**

- the "story" is a product description with a history paragraph in front of it;
- it is really about a brand or a product range;
- it uses generic praise or trivia where a concrete observation belongs;
- it states a popular legend as history, or a technical fact that is not true or cannot be checked;
- interesting writing hides a missing or unchecked fact;
- it makes health or therapy claims ("lieči stres", "zlepšuje pamäť");
- it repeats a live article's angle (slow life, stress relief, the history of wooden puzzles in general) instead of a new one.

## `GIFT`

**Purpose.** Help someone choose a gift for a specific person and a specific day, well enough that the gift gets built and not shelved. The reader leaves knowing how to choose, not just what to click.

**Reader.** The giver, who often does not build: a daughter before Mother's Day, a partner before Valentine's Day, a parent before Children's Day. They know the recipient, not the hobby.

**Example topics:**

- Deň otcov: čo darovať otcovi, ktorý rád niečo vyrába rukami
- Deň detí: ako vybrať stavebnicu podľa veku a trpezlivosti dieťaťa
- Deň starých rodičov: projekt, ktorý poskladajú spolu s vnúčatami

**A good article answers** by connecting what the giver knows about the person to a criterion and to what to check on the kit. It also answers:

- how to match a kit to the recipient: patience, time, hands, space, age, and whether they want decoration, a mechanism, light, or movement;
- what to check before buying: piece count, assembly time, difficulty, recommended age, finished size, glue, tools;
- how to give it so it gets built: together, in stages, with a small set of tools;
- when the day is, so the reader has time to prepare.

A parameter that is not turned into a choice does not count. „Ak rád pracuje na jednom projekte viac večerov, vyšší počet dielov nemusí vadiť. Pozri aj čas, náročnosť a rozmery“ passes. „Väčší model je skvelý darček“ fails.

**It fails when:**

- it is a list of products with one sentence each;
- the choosing advice would be the same for any recipient, or it assumes the giver is the builder;
- it uses fake urgency, discount language, price talk, or an empty phrase such as „dokonalý darček“;
- it is tied to no day in the holiday calendar, or to a state holiday;
- a year in the title ages it ("najlepšie darčeky 2026");
- the products can be removed and no choosing logic remains.

A `GIFT` article may carry more product content than other pillars, within the limits in [`09-editorial-guidelines.md`](09-editorial-guidelines.md). It still passes the removed-products test: without the products, the reader still knows how to choose.

## `COMMUNITY`

**Purpose.** Show builders to each other, so readers come back for people and not only for products. The reader leaves having seen what others made and how.

**Reader.** A builder who takes part in the monthly challenge or follows it, and a newcomer deciding whether this hobby has people behind it.

**Example topics:**

- Výsledky septembrovej výzvy: vlastné úpravy, ktoré ste nám poslali
- Stavba mesiaca: book nook s vlastným osvetlením krok za krokom
- Čo hovoria stavitelia: chyby pri prvom modeli, na ktoré nás upozornili

The article may use only what the supplied material supports. Do not invent names, quotes, results, numbers, motivations, steps, reactions, or a lesson the builder did not give and the material does not show. Use the builder's words when the material contains them. Do not smooth a quote, lengthen it, or turn a review into one.

**A good article answers:**

- what was made, and what is actually visible or written in the material;
- how they did the part that stands out, when the material supports the method, so another builder can try it;
- what worked or failed, when the material says so;
- how the reader can take part next time, when that is relevant.

**It fails when:**

- it names or quotes a person without a consent line in the material ([`community/`](../community/README.md));
- it invents a builder, a result, a number, a motivation, a step, or a quote, or it improves a quote that is in the material;
- it is a winner announcement plus a shop link;
- it paraphrases a review and presents it as a quote;
- it could have been written without the supplied material, or it replaces a documented detail with generic praise.

## Borderline cases

Decide by **what the reader leaves with**, not by which words appear in the topic.

**`GUIDE` vs. `GIFT` on a first model.** "Ako vybrať prvú stavebnicu pre seba" is `GUIDE`. "Čo darovať začiatočníkovi na Deň otcov" is `GIFT`, because the reader is the giver and the day sets the date.

**`GUIDE` vs. `INSPIRATION` on a mechanism.** Explaining how a music box or a gearbox works is `INSPIRATION`. Fixing one that sticks is `GUIDE`.

**`INSPIRATION` vs. `GIFT` on a holiday.** A holiday row may be either. "Spoločné tvorenie s mamou" is `INSPIRATION` and does not count against the one-`GIFT`-per-week limit. It becomes `GIFT` only when choosing a present is the reader's question.

**`INSPIRATION` vs. `COMMUNITY` on a builder's story.** A story about a named builder is `COMMUNITY` and needs the Editor's material. Without it, write about the technique, not the person.

**`GUIDE` vs. `INSPIRATION` on display.** „Ako zavesiť model rovno“ is `GUIDE`. „Ako svetlo a pozadie menia hotový model na poličke“ is `INSPIRATION`.

**`GUIDE` vs. `COMMUNITY` on a modification.** „Ako pridať osvetlenie do book nook“ is `GUIDE` when general technical guidance supports it. „Ako Peter pridal osvetlenie do svojho book nook“ is `COMMUNITY` only when Peter and his method are in the approved material.

## Out of identity

These topics belong to no pillar. Planner does not plan them and Creator does not write them. When a plan row asks for one, stop the run and name the row and the topic.

- Legislation, regulations, safety standards, and customs or tax rules.
- State holidays and days off as such: national and civic anniversaries, and what is open or closed on a public holiday. An international or commemorative day in the holiday calendar is allowed only when the article serves the `GIFT` or `INSPIRATION` rules. An article about the holiday itself is out of identity.
- Discount announcements, sale events, price comparisons, and stock or delivery notices.
- An article whose subject is a brand or a product range.
- Politics, news, and anything that needs a source the bots cannot check.

## Adding a pillar

A pillar is added here, in [`13-planner.md`](13-planner.md), and in [`09-editorial-guidelines.md`](09-editorial-guidelines.md) together, by a person. A bot never invents a pillar or files a row under a pillar not in the overview table.
